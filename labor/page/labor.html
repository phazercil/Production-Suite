Labor Allocation — Labor Stages

Each position's labor is charged to one of these stages. Machines still make Stage 1 or Stage 2 units, but every labor stage's cost is divided by total units (= Stage 1 units, since Stage 2/3 work modifies those same units). The stage costs add up to the overall Labor $ / Unit.

"Match Line's Stage" Positions

Manufacturing Line Totals & Accountant Overtime Reports

Stage 1 lines produce base units. Stage 2 lines process Stage 1 units. Each line computes its own Labor $/Unit based on its stage output.

Section 1A: Labor Cost by Labor Stage

Each position's labor goes to the labor stage set on page 2. Every stage is divided by total units (= Stage 1 units), so the stage costs add up to the overall Labor $ / Unit.

Section 1B: Labor by Production Line

Each line's labor split across labor stages. Example: a Stage 1 line's operators count as Stage 1 labor, while its packers count as Packaging labor.

Section 1C: Labor by Shift

All lines combined, split by shift and labor stage.

Section 1D: Month-by-Month Labor by Labor Stage

Always the full year, using the labor cost basis set on page 6.
